UC3: Procurement package formation

From a plain-language specification to structured procurement requirements.

The quality of a bid comparison depends entirely on the quality of the input. Dundir's AI layer translates unstructured specifications into the structured requirements the optimisation engine needs.

The problem

Procurement professionals write specifications in plain language, or receive them that way from the project team. "500 m² of tiling, BRL-9010 certified, delivery before week 14" is a perfectly adequate description for a human, but not usable input for a comparison algorithm.

Hamppi's research at Skanska demonstrates this directly: for simple, structured bids, generative AI performed well. For complex, unstructured bids, the calculations failed. The dominant variable was the quality and uniformity of the input data, rather than the model itself.

Skanska finding: input determines everything

"Skanska should look into the possibility of demanding suppliers to fill their bids into a standardised bidding template, because this would increase the accuracy of GenAI-based comparisons." Hamppi's recommendation directly confirms that standardised input is the critical operational dependency, rather than the model alone.

Hamppi 2025, p. 69: Aalto University / Skanska Talonrakennus Oy.

How Dundir forms procurement packages

  1. Read the specification

    The AI layer reads the project specification in plain language (from a PDF, ERP export, or email). Relevant procurement requirements are extracted and structured.

  2. Define hard requirements and weighted objectives

    The system produces a draft list: which certifications are mandatory? What is the maximum delivery window? What is the budget ceiling? The buyer verifies and adjusts where needed.

  3. Export the structured procurement package

    The resulting package is the input for bid comparison (UC1) and supplier evaluation (UC2). It can also be sent to suppliers as a standardised invitation template.
