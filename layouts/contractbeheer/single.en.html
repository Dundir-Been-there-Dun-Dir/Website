UC4: Contract oversight

The contract is signed. The work isn't done.

Dundir records the award decision and monitors supplier performance against commitments: certification expiry dates, delivery variances, and an audit trail covering both execution and selection.

The problem

A contractor awards a contract to supplier X on the basis of a certified bid, a promised delivery window, and a sustainability score. Three months later: the delivery is two weeks late, the KOMO certificate has lapsed, and the material specification diverges from the bid.

Who tracked this? In most construction companies: nobody systematically. The buyer remembers, or records disappear into email threads. When an audit arrives or a defect surfaces, there is no verifiable trail.

For municipalities, this is not an inconvenience. It is legal exposure. The Aanbestedingswet requires demonstrable documentation of supplier selection. EU CPR 2024 and CSRD Scope 3 impose traceability requirements on materials through the supply chain. That traceability starts at the contract and extends through physical delivery.

What contract oversight means in practice

Certification monitoring
KOMO, SKG-IKOB, and other certifications have expiry dates. Dundir tracks validity per supplier and per contract, issuing alerts before certifications lapse during the execution of an assignment.

Delivery time tracking
Promised delivery windows are compared against actual deliveries. Deviations are logged per assignment and per supplier, and feed into supplier scores for future tenders.

Specification conformance
Does the delivered material conform to the specification in the procurement package? Deviations are documented. When a technical inspection or audit arrives, the trail is already there.

Execution audit trail
Every contract phase (award, delivery, inspection, payment) is logged with date, responsible party, and any deviation. The file the auditor or procurement supervisor asks for already exists.

Why this is a legal requirement for municipalities

For a mid-size contractor, contract oversight is a matter of efficiency and knowledge retention. For a municipality, it is a legal requirement.

The Aanbestedingswet requires that every award decision be demonstrably defensible to internal leadership, municipal councils, the audit chamber, and the Authority for Consumers and Markets. That defensibility does not stop at the award. If a municipality selects a supplier on the basis of a sustainability score and that supplier does not perform to that score, the documentation of the deviation is as important as the original selection documentation.

There are 342 Dutch municipalities with near-identical procurement processes and near-identical compliance challenges. Dundir produces the required audit trail across each contract phase automatically, without manual administrative overhead.

Regulatory context

EU CPR 2024 requires demonstrable documentation of construction product performance through the supply chain. CSRD Directive 2022/2464/EU requires large organisations to report their Scope 3 emissions, including those of purchased materials and services. Aanbestedingswet article 2.130 requires a reasoned award decision that is verifiable. None of these requirements stop at supplier selection. They apply to contract execution as well.

Contract oversight closes the procurement loop

The four use cases form a closed loop together: procurement package formation (UC3) → bid comparison (UC1) → supplier evaluation (UC2) → contract oversight (UC4). Every execution data point from UC4 improves the supplier scores in UC2, which in turn inform the next bid comparison in UC1.

A system that becomes more accurate after every project. That retains the knowledge of departing buyers. And that documents every phase of every tender: from initial specification through final delivery.
