Why local, not cloud

  • The vendor may not be there for year 5 of the contract. OpenAI, the most visible name in AI, posted a $20.9B operating loss against $13.1B revenue in 2025 - leaked financials independently verified by the Financial Times (Zitron 2026). No frontier AI lab has yet proven this business model holds at the scale a public body would depend on
  • The model you build against will not stay still. Anthropic commits to a minimum 60 days’ notice before retiring a model - Claude Sonnet 4 and Opus 4 were gone 62 days after notice, June 2026 (Anthropic 2026). OpenAI gives 6 months for production models, as little as 2 weeks for preview ones (OpenAI 2026). A multi-year contract means repeated forced migrations, on the vendor’s calendar
  • The compliance basis for “cloud is fine” has already collapsed once. The EU-US Data Privacy Framework survived its first court challenge in September 2025, but it replaced Privacy Shield - which the same court struck down in 2020 - and is already under appeal (General Court of the European Union 2025)

$20.9B OpenAI operating loss, 2025, on $13.1B revenue (Zitron 2026)

None of this is a technical gap a cloud vendor can promise around. A model that runs on your own server doesn’t need a competitor’s balance sheet to hold, its deprecation calendar to skip you, or a legal framework that’s already fallen once to survive the next challenge.

This reframes local deployment from a compliance checkbox into a risk-management argument - the numbers are cited and dated, not FUD, and they describe the market generally, not a dig at one named vendor. For a finance- or procurement-literate audience this lands harder than privacy alone: a ten-year public contract can’t depend on a vendor’s burn rate, deprecation cadence, or a legal basis that’s already been struck down once before.

Are you sure

  • Most organisations lack structured tracking of delivery and compliance data - the audit trail is retroactive document chasing
  • The Aanbestedingswet and EU Directive 2014/24 don’t accept “we meant to document it” (Rijksoverheid 2024)
