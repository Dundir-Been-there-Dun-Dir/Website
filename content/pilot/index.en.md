slug: pilot
lede: 'The system is not finished. That is not small print, that is the offer: you help decide what it gets calibrated on, and after handover you own what stands.'
sections:
- eyebrow: Why a design partner
  heading: A generic model does not know your suppliers.
  body: 'The moat on this product is not an algorithm. It is the calibration: knowing which certifications are hard requirements for you, which supplier delivers more reliably despite a higher price, and which deviation from the specification is acceptable in practice. None of that is in a handbook. It is in your procurement history.


    So at this stage we work with a small number of design partners rather than as many customers as possible. Whoever joins now joins while the shape can still change.'
- tone: chalk
  eyebrow: The track
  heading: Four phases, one handover.
  steps:
  - h: Intake and process mapping
    p: 'We map your current procurement process: which systems, which suppliers, which decision criteria, and where approval comes from. This is what tells us whether a layer on your existing software makes more sense than a separate deployment.'
  - h: Data intake and fine-tuning
    p: The system is trained on your ERP procurement history and supplier data. This is what makes the output specific to your organisation instead of generic to the sector.
  - h: Deployment on your infrastructure
    p: Locally, on your own servers. No cloud connection required. Your data does not leave your network, not even during training.
  - h: 'Handover: you own the system outright'
    p: After handover no subscription is required for it to run. Maintenance and retraining are bought when you find them useful, not because the system stops otherwise.
- eyebrow: Terms
  heading: What each side puts on the table.
  claims:
  - fig: You bring
    body: Access to procurement files and to the buyer doing the daily work, not only to their manager. That buyer's time, on the order of a few hours a month. And the willingness to say when the output is wrong.
    word: true
  - fig: We bring
    body: Deployment, calibration on your data, and the handover after which you own the system. A non-disclosure agreement before any data moves, and a written data boundary setting out what the system touches and who can reach it.
    word: true
  - fig: You decide
    body: The system advises and documents. The buyer reviews and signs. Responsibility for the procurement decision stays with your organisation, and we put that in the contract too.
    word: true
  caveat:
    lab: What we cannot promise yet
    text: We have no pilot data, so we cannot guarantee a saving. The research supports the problem and the local deployment model. It does not prove that this approach buys more cheaply in practice than your best buyer. That is exactly what a pilot has to establish, and anyone joining knows that up front.

  id: apply
  eyebrow: Apply
  heading: See whether it fits.
  body: A pilot starts with a conversation, not a quote. Tell us briefly what kind of organisation you are, which procurement problem costs you most, and who decides on procurement tooling.
