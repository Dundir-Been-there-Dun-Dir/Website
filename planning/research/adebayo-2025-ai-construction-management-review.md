## Core argument

AI adoption in construction project management is accelerating -- driven by technology availability, productivity pressure, competitive advantage, and sustainability goals -- but remains behind other industries due to persistent structural barriers including data fragmentation, skills gaps, high implementation costs, and resistance to change. The review maps the full arc from expert systems (1970s--2010) through machine learning dominance (post-COVID) to emerging future trends in digital twins, optimisation, and autonomous construction.

## Key findings relevant to the business

- **Planning and Monitoring/Control are the dominant phases for AI application.** Planning is the single most utilised lifecycle phase across all three industrial periods studied. This directly maps to where procurement decisions are made.
- **Optimisation is an established and growing AI purpose in construction.** 22 of 135 papers (16%) focused on optimisation; 8 of 64 post-COVID papers (12.5%) focused specifically on optimisation tools, indicating "delayed but growing recognition of their potential value in construction" (p. 8).
- **Decision support is the most common AI purpose overall**, cited in 28 of 135 papers, followed equally by performance improvement (28) and predictive analytics (28). Automation (14) and optimisation (22) round out the top categories.
- **Deep learning for bidding and pricing is explicitly identified** in Table 1 (p. 5) as a documented optimisation application -- "Deep learning for optimising pricing and bidding" -- alongside resource management and information management.
- **Machine learning dominates and is accelerating.** ML appeared in 11/37 (29.7%) of papers during Industry 3.0, rising to 47/64 (73.4%) during the post-COVID period. This is the fastest-growing technology category in construction AI research.
- **Optimisation tools are most useful in planning and execution phases** "where they are mostly needed for efficiency such as cost savings, process improvement, and optimal resource utilisation" (p. 9).
- **Construction is a confirmed laggard vs. other industries.** "AI adoption in construction projects remains relatively limited compared to other sectors, such as finance and manufacturing" (p. 2, citing Regona et al. 2022).
- **COVID-19 accelerated AI adoption.** The post-COVID era produced 66 of 135 papers -- more than the previous two industrial periods combined. The pandemic "accelerated the digital transformation from Industry 4.0, making industries more open to the use of technology" (p. 6).
- **Resistance to change is structural, not incidental.** Construction "has thrived on the use of conventional methods that make it risk-averse, resistant to perceived drastic changes" and shows "organisational inertia, and lag in AI adoption in comparison to other industries" (p. 13--14).
- **High implementation costs deter SMEs specifically.** "These costs tend to be a deterrent, especially to small- and medium-sized enterprises" (p. 14, citing Tang et al. 2009).
- **Data in construction is structurally problematic.** "Data extracted from the construction industry tends to be unstructured, fragmented, and incomplete" and "historical data are also a major problem as records are often unavailable or collected inconsistently" (p. 14).
- **NLP has emerging procurement relevance.** NLP was used "to extract contract change reasons and altered work items for advanced retrieval of change orders" (ref. 30, Ko et al. 2021) and for "intelligent system for the acquisition and management of information from bill of quantities in building projects" (ref. 102, Martinez-Rojas et al. 2016).
- **Knowledge-based systems for contractor selection are documented.** Table 1 (p. 5) lists "graph theory and matrix methods for contractor selection" under decision support, and "deep learning for contractor selection and manager selection."
- **Local deployment aligns with the interoperability problem.** The paper identifies that "transitioning from traditional systems may be hindered by the lack of compatibility between traditional or existing systems and AI systems" as a technical shortcoming (p. 14) -- a strong rationale for locally-integrated rather than cloud-only architectures.
- **Europe is a secondary region in AI-construction research** with only 27 of 135 papers (20%), behind Asia Pacific (59, 44%) and North America (30, 22%). European construction firms are likely technology followers, not leaders -- suggesting a market that needs practical tools rather than cutting-edge research.

## Useful statistics and data points

- 135 peer-reviewed articles reviewed, spanning 1985--2024 (p. 1, p. 5)
- Industry 3.0 (1970--early 2010): 38 papers; Industry 4.0 (2010--2020): 31 papers; Industry 4.0 post-COVID (2020--2024): 66 papers (p. 5--6)
- 69/135 papers (51%) used computational experiments; 40/135 (29.6%) were case studies (p. 6)
- AI type breakdown: Machine learning featured in 77 studies; Knowledge-based systems in 21; Optimisation in 13; NLP in 6; Basic (non-AI) tools in 18 (p. 6)
- ML adoption jumped from 11/37 (29.7%) in 3IR to 47/64 (73.4%) in 4IR PC (p. 8)
- Optimisation research in construction: 8/64 (12.5%) of post-COVID papers (p. 8)
- AI purpose breakdown (of 135 papers): Decision support 28, Automation 14, Performance improvement 28, Optimisation 22, Predictive 28, Forecasting 10, Evaluation 5 (p. 7)
- Planning phase AI purposes: predictive functions 22.6%, optimisation 20%, decision support 12.6%, performance improvement 24% (p. 10)
- Monitoring and control phase AI purposes: predictive functions 22%, decision support 25% (p. 10)
- ML in planning phase: cited in 43/79 (54%) of articles focusing on planning; in monitoring and control: 25/37 (68%) of articles (p. 9)
- Regional research breakdown: Asia Pacific 59/135 (44%), North America 30/135 (22%), Europe 27/135 (20%), Middle East & Africa 17/135 (13%), Latin America 2/135 (1%) (p. 11--12)
- North America dominated the global AI construction market in 2023 (p. 11, citing Precedence Research 2024)

## Methodology

Structured literature review following Mayring's four-step process model: (1) material collection, (2) descriptive analysis, (3) category selection, (4) material evaluation. Sources: Scopus (353), Web of Science (673), ProQuest (411) -- total 1,437 initially identified, filtered to 135 peer-reviewed English-language journal articles through five sequential filters (language, journal type, duplicates, abstract, full text). Thematic coding used to identify AI types, purposes, and lifecycle phases. Quantitative visualisation applied to frequency counts across themes.

## Applicability to our project

**Market thesis.** The paper provides peer-reviewed confirmation that (a) construction AI is accelerating, (b) optimisation and decision support in the planning phase are the most researched and validated use cases, and (c) bidding/pricing optimisation is an explicitly documented AI application. This is strong academic backing for the product's core function.

**AI adoption framing.** The documented jump from 29.7% ML adoption (3IR) to 73.4% (4IR PC) gives a credible narrative of acceleration. The industry is moving -- but slowly -- which positions the company as entering at the right inflection point with the right technology.

**Grant positioning.** The review's emphasis on barriers -- data fragmentation, skills gaps, SME cost sensitivity, interoperability -- maps directly to the kinds of problems EU and national innovation grants fund solutions for. Framing the product as addressing documented, peer-reviewed barriers (rather than hypothetical ones) strengthens grant applications. The sustainability and energy efficiency focus (Section 5.1) also aligns with green transition grant themes.

**Barriers we need to address.** Four barriers are directly relevant to product design and go-to-market:
1. Resistance to change / unfamiliarity with AI tools -- points to need for human-in-the-loop design and explainability
2. Skills gap -- points to need for a UI that does not require data science literacy
3. High initial costs / SME deterrent -- supports SaaS or usage-based pricing over large upfront licences
4. Data quality and fragmentation -- points to the value of local fine-tuned models that work with the contractor's own historical data, not generic cloud models

**Local deployment rationale.** The identified limitation of "lack of compatibility between traditional or existing systems and AI systems" supports the architectural decision to deploy locally and integrate with existing workflows rather than require cloud data sharing, which also addresses the data privacy barrier.

## Limitations / caveats

- **Procurement is not studied as a distinct phase.** The construction lifecycle used in this paper is: initiation, planning, execution, monitoring and control, handover. Procurement is not carved out separately -- it falls within "planning." Citing this paper as evidence of AI in "procurement specifically" requires inference, not direct claim.
- **No primary data collected.** All 135 source papers involved computational experiments or case studies; this review only synthesises them. Findings are second-order and should be cited as such.
- **Optimisation is underdeveloped in construction research.** Only 13 studies focused on optimisation as the primary AI type (p. 6), and only 8/64 post-COVID papers (12.5%) addressed optimisation tools. The market validation is real but the research base is thin -- framing this as an emerging rather than mature field is more defensible.
- **Asia Pacific dominates the research base** (44% of papers), much of it driven by Chinese infrastructure policy. Findings about barriers and drivers may not translate directly to the European construction market, which accounts for only 20% of papers.
- **No procurement-specific cost or ROI data.** The paper does not include quantified ROI or cost-saving data from AI implementation in procurement contexts. Any ROI claims in pitches must come from other sources.
- **Basic (non-AI) tools count inflates "AI adoption" framing.** 18 of 135 papers (13%) studied tools like Excel categorised as "basic non-AI." This is included in the review's scope, which means some of the "AI adoption" narrative actually describes digitisation, not AI. Read the primary sources before citing specific sub-claims.
- **Industry 4.0 post-COVID period (2020--2024) is only 4 years** but accounts for 49% of papers, which may reflect publication lag and recency bias in database indexing rather than a genuine productivity surge.
