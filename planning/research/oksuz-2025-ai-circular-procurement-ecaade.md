## Core argument

AI has significant potential to advance circular procurement (CP) in construction through data-driven decision-making, resource optimisation, and supply chain management, but adoption is constrained by data gaps, legislative immaturity, high upfront costs, and a shortage of technical expertise. The paper calls for purpose-built AI platforms that embed circularity into procurement processes dynamically and responsively.

## Key findings relevant to the business

- Research on AI in circular construction is heavily skewed toward design and materials; research specifically on **procurement operations** is explicitly identified as a gap: "research on construction procurement operations remains limited" (p. 219).
- AI's core value in this domain is framed as **decision support through compressing large complex datasets into manageable form** -- directly analogous to the optimization/decision-engine framing of the project (p. 218).
- The paper distinguishes two AI capacities relevant to circular business models: **augmentation** (e.g., optimisation solutions) and **automation** (e.g., autonomous solutions) -- citing Raisch and Krakowski (2021). An optimization-based procurement tool maps cleanly onto the augmentation category.
- Six thematic areas where AI is being applied: (1) CE policy and strategy, (2) circular material/product design, (3) circular construction and buildings, (4) circular supply chains and manufacturing, (5) CDW management, (6) waste management and resource recovery. Supply chain management (theme 4) is the closest match to construction procurement; it is the second-largest cluster with 18 of 108 papers.
- The largest AI application cluster in the SLR is **CDW management** (35 papers), followed by **waste management and resource recovery** (29 papers) -- indicating where AI in construction is currently most mature, not in procurement itself.
- For supply chain AI, the literature covers blockchain-based traceability and product passports, predictive logistics, reverse logistics via ML, and Industry 4.0 digital manufacturing. These are upstream of where a procurement decision-engine would sit.
- **Localised datasets** are identified as critical: imported AI tools fail when not adapted to local material profiles and construction standards (explicitly noted for Türkiye, p. 222). This is a direct argument for the local fine-tuned model strategy.
- Platform-level examples cited: YardLink (UK) connects contractors with suppliers to streamline procurement and enhance supply chain visibility; inSuppliers (Türkiye) connects construction stakeholders with sustainability filters and verified supplier data for early-stage decision-making. These are the closest competitive analogues mentioned.
- The study calls explicitly for "AI platforms that facilitate the adoption of circular construction practices, supporting the development of dynamic, responsive, and smart procurement strategies" (abstract) -- framing the product category as an open research and market need.

## Useful statistics and data points

- SLR corpus: 349 records initially retrieved, reduced to 108 documents included in full review (p. 219-220).
- Distribution of 108 papers across themes: CDW management: 35; Waste management and resource recovery: 29; Circular supply chains and manufacturing: 18; Circular construction and buildings: 8; Circular material and product design: 4; CE policy and strategy: 14 (p. 220, Figure 3).
- UK 2025 parliamentary committee report: over **60% of government bodies cited poor data quality** and **70% reported AI talent shortages** as key barriers to AI adoption in the public sector (p. 222).
- Türkiye AI legislation proposal submitted June 2024 but lacks risk classifications and clear definitions for high-risk AI systems (p. 222).

## Methodology

Systematic Literature Review (SLR) following PRISMA protocol. Databases: Scopus (147), IEEE Xplore (84), ScienceDirect (52). Boolean search combining circular procurement / circular economy terms with construction terms and AI/ML terms. After deduplication and screening, 108 papers were included. Thematic clustering used VOSviewer for keyword co-occurrence visualisation, then a rule-based Python script to classify papers into six themes based on title/abstract metadata. Followed by qualitative comparative analysis of UK vs. Türkiye on policy readiness, technological adoption, institutional capacity, and practical applications.

## Quotes worth using

- "The fundamental benefit of AI is creating a platform for decision support through valuable insights and results collected from large and complex data sets and compressed into a manageable form." (p. 218)
- "Most research on AI in circular construction focuses on enhancing design and construction practices, optimising resource utilisation, and promoting sustainability, whereas research on construction procurement operations remains limited." (p. 219)
- "The findings emphasise the need for AI platforms that facilitate the adoption of circular construction practices, supporting the development of dynamic, responsive, and smart procurement strategies." (abstract / p. 217)
- "There is also a lack of localised datasets for AI training, limiting the performance of imported AI tools that are not adapted to Türkiye's material profiles and construction standards." (p. 222)
- "While the UK exhibits higher institutional readiness, supported by clear policy frameworks and active pilot programs, practical implementation remains fragmented." (conclusion, p. 223)

## Applicability to our project

**Procurement AI positioning:** The paper is the strongest peer-reviewed citation available for the claim that AI-in-procurement is an underserved research and product gap. It names the need and fails to identify any deployed solution that fully addresses it -- which is the opportunity.

**Sustainability and circular framing:** The paper connects procurement AI directly to CSRD-relevant outcomes: waste reduction, material lifecycle extension, CDW minimisation, resource recovery. This gives the product a credible sustainability narrative without having to manufacture one.

**Local model strategy validation:** The explicit finding that imported AI tools underperform due to lack of localised training data (material profiles, construction standards) directly supports the local fine-tuned model architecture. This is a peer-reviewed argument for why a locally adapted model is not just a nice-to-have but a functional requirement.

**Barriers to address in product design and GTM:** Data quality, interoperability between AI platforms and fragmented data systems, high upfront costs for SMEs, and AI talent shortage are all named barriers. The product should address: (a) low-friction data onboarding, (b) pricing accessible to SME contractors, (c) no requirement for in-house AI expertise to operate.

**CSRD/circular framing:** The six-theme taxonomy (especially themes 1, 4, and 5) maps onto CSRD reporting requirements for supply chain sustainability and waste. A procurement tool generating structured output on material circularity, supplier sustainability filters, and waste traceability could be positioned as CSRD-adjacent infrastructure.

**Competitive landscape:** YardLink and inSuppliers are cited as the closest analogues to a procurement AI platform. Neither appears to include an optimization-based decision engine -- they are primarily supplier directories with sustainability tagging. The optimization layer is the differentiator.

## Limitations / caveats

- This is a **systematic literature review**, not primary research. It maps what exists in the academic literature; it does not measure market adoption, commercial readiness, or user behaviour.
- The comparative analysis of UK vs. Türkiye is qualitative and descriptive, not quantitative. The 60%/70% statistics on data quality and AI talent shortages come from a UK parliamentary committee report (2025), not from this paper's own data collection.
- The paper **does not provide a quantitative assessment of the effectiveness or maturity** of the AI applications it discusses (acknowledged in the conclusion, p. 223).
- The SLR is limited to English-language published documents and may miss undocumented or non-digitised initiatives, especially from Türkiye's informal sector.
- The claim that "research on procurement operations remains limited" is relative to the 108-paper corpus; there may be practitioner literature, grey literature, or non-English academic work not captured.
- The paper was published in 2025 but references a Türkiye AI law proposal from June 2024 as current -- the regulatory situation may have evolved.
- No user studies, interviews, or demand-side validation of AI procurement tools. The paper argues from supply-side (research gaps) and policy-side, not from verified buyer need.
